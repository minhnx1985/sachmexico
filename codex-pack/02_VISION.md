# 02 — VISUAL VISION

## Principle

**Editorial Mexico, not souvenir Mexico.**

Do not use clichés as the primary design language:
- sombreros;
- cactus icons;
- papel picado borders;
- mariachi silhouettes;
- flag stripes everywhere;
- generic “magical realism” visuals.

Instead, draw from:
- Mexican editorial modernism;
- literary journals and art-book typography;
- printmaking / paper texture;
- restrained geometry;
- the burgundy identity in the supplied Embassy image;
- deep green from Nhã Nam;
- cream, ink black, ochre and earth tones.

## Global palette
Suggested starting point, derive exact values after inspecting supplied logos and local cover assets:
- paper: warm ivory
- ink: near-black
- Mexico burgundy: deep wine red
- Nhã Nam green: deep botanical green
- ochre/gold: restrained diplomatic accent
- clay/terracotta: small editorial accent

Do not make the page look like a Mexican-flag campaign.

## Typography
Use at most two font families:
- a high-character editorial display serif with Vietnamese support;
- a clean text serif/sans with Vietnamese support.

Large typographic composition is encouraged.

## Author micro-worlds
Each author can have a subtle system while remaining in one editorial language:

### Octavio Paz
maze / grid / void / intellectual geometry.

### Carlos Fuentes
shadow / doorway / doubled forms / Gothic darkness.

### Alberto Ruy-Sánchez
wind / water / earth / sensory linework / Mogador geometry.

### Juan Villoro
bookshelf / paper / moving pages / reading labyrinth.

### Cristina Rivera Garza
archive / letters / files / fragments / documentary typography.

These should be CSS/editorial motifs, not AI illustrations.

## Motion
Restrained:
- typographic reveal;
- subtle cover lift;
- author-module transitions;
- lightbox.

No autoplay carousel.
Honor reduced motion.
