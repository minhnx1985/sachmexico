AN INVITATION TO READ

One literature,
many Mexicos.

These five authors do not belong to a single literary school.

Mexico has many faces in literature: Octavio Paz’s questions about national identity; Carlos Fuentes’s seductive, haunted house; Alberto Ruy-Sánchez’s city of desire and the senses; Juan Villoro’s library where books choose their readers; and Cristina Rivera Garza’s painful search for justice.

Through works already available or forthcoming in Vietnamese from Nhã Nam, we invite readers to meet five very different voices in Mexican literature, from twentieth-century classics to one of today’s important contemporary writers.

Which door will you open?

01 / BOOKS & ADVENTURESBorn in 1956

Writer · Journalist

Juan Villoro

A Mexican writer invites readers into a world where books have lives of their own.

Born in Mexico City in 1956, Juan Villoro is a writer, journalist and one of the most versatile voices in contemporary Mexican literature. His work spans novels, short stories, plays, essays, journalism and writing for children. He has received literary and journalism awards in Mexico, Spain and Latin America.

02 / IDENTITY & SOLITUDE1914–1998

Nobel Prize in Literature 1990

Octavio Paz

To see Mexico from within, this is one of the books to turn to.

Octavio Paz (1914–1998) was a Mexican poet, essayist and diplomat, and a major figure in twentieth-century Spanish-language literature. He spent many years in the Mexican diplomatic service, including a term as ambassador to India. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1990.

03 / DESIRE & THE SENSESBorn in 1951

Premio Nacional de Artes y Literatura 2017

Alberto Ruy-Sánchez

Mogador: a geography of desire, where the body, memory and the elements find their voices.

Born in Mexico City in 1951, Alberto Ruy-Sánchez is a novelist, poet, essayist and editor. With Margarita de Orellana, he leads the cultural project Artes de México. His Quinteto de Mogador turns the city of Mogador into a geography of desire, the body, memory and the senses. In 2017 he received Mexico’s Premio Nacional de Artes y Literatura.

04 / MEMORY & SHADOWS1928–2012

Premio Cervantes 1987

Carlos Fuentes

A dark house, a young woman, a past that keeps repeating itself.

Carlos Fuentes (1928–2012) was a Mexican novelist, essayist and diplomat, and a central figure in the Latin American Boom. A member of El Colegio Nacional and a former Mexican ambassador to France, he received the Cervantes Prize in 1987.

Forthcoming in Vietnam from Nhã Nam

Pulitzer Prize 2024 · Memoir or Autobiography

Cristina Rivera Garza

Liliana’s
Invincible Summer

A Sister’s Search for Justice

Restoring Liliana’s life beyond the case file.

Drawing on letters, notebooks, police files, family memories and the accounts of those who loved Liliana, Rivera Garza restores her sister’s voice, friendships, plans and interrupted youth. Liliana emerges as a person with a life of her own, beyond the label of a victim in a crime file.

About Cristina Rivera Garza

Cristina Rivera Garza is a Mexican writer, poet and scholar who teaches at the University of Houston. Her work crosses the boundaries between fiction, history, documents, memory and formal experimentation. A MacArthur Fellow and two-time recipient of the Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz Prize, she won the 2024 Pulitzer Prize for Memoir or Autobiography for Liliana’s Invincible Summer.
